# SYMBIOSIS --- PROJECT MASTER SPEC v3.1

**Status:** Architecture source of truth for the next implementation.\
**Scope:** Software + hardware prototype.\
**Purpose:** Build a vendor-neutral, human-in-the-loop **continuous risk
verification platform** for property insurers and insured businesses.\
**Build model:** Empty Git repository → clone locally → VS Code → Claude
extension/Claude Code → implement one bounded phase at a time.\
**Cloud target:** Google Cloud using available project credits.\
**Prototype hardware:** ESP32 + SHT41 + MPU6050 + INA219 + low-voltage
fans/motor/MOSFETs/button/switch.\
**Important:** This document supersedes `PROJECT SPEC v2.1` where they
differ. The existing detailed hardware bring-up/firmware roadmap remains
useful as an implementation companion, but its contracts must conform to
this v3.0 architecture.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 0. EXECUTIVE ARCHITECTURE DECISION

Symbiosis is **not primarily an IoT alerting product** and is **not an
autonomous underwriting system**.

Its core product is a **Risk Improvement Verification Platform**:

> **Sensors detect → deterministic logic establishes the risk state → AI
> explains → people act → sensors verify → evidence is assembled → the
> customer controls what the insurer sees → continued monitoring can
> reopen the case if the risk returns.**

The central domain object is therefore **not an Alert**. It is a
persistent **Risk Improvement Case** that links:

1.  a risk-control recommendation or newly detected hazard,
2.  trusted physical observations,
3.  a risk event,
4.  accountable human action,
5.  post-action verification,
6.  an evidence package,
7.  consented insurer sharing,
8.  continued monitoring and possible recurrence.

Alerts are communications generated by a case. They are not the case
itself.

# PART I --- PRODUCT AND RESEARCH POSITIONING

## 1. PRODUCT THESIS

Existing insurer and property-IoT programs already demonstrate that
sensors can detect water, freeze, temperature and equipment conditions
and notify people. Symbiosis should not claim that sensor alerting
itself is novel.

The product hypothesis to test is:

> **The weak link is often what happens after risk is identified: who
> owns the recommendation, whether the action was completed, whether the
> action actually reduced the physical risk, whether the improvement
> persisted, and what trustworthy evidence can be shared with the
> insurer.**

Symbiosis closes that loop without requiring the insurer to consume
continuous raw telemetry.

### 1.1 One-line positioning

**Symbiosis turns physical risk signals and human mitigation actions
into sensor-verified, auditable risk-improvement evidence that the
insured controls and can choose to share with its insurer.**

### 1.2 Demo line

**AI explains. People act. Sensors verify. Evidence proves. The customer
controls what the insurer sees.**

### 1.3 What Symbiosis does not claim

-   It does not claim that an alert means a loss was prevented.
-   It does not automatically change premiums.
-   It does not autonomously underwrite, bind, cancel, renew, or
    adjudicate claims.
-   It does not autonomously command building equipment in this
    prototype.
-   It does not claim actuarial validation.
-   It does not claim that a synthetic benchmark equals a real carrier
    pilot.
-   It does not claim that every equipment failure can be predicted.
-   It does not call a user-reported repair "verified."
-   It does not expose raw insured telemetry to an insurer by default.
-   It does not use an LLM to decide whether a physical risk was fixed.

## 2. PRIMARY PERSONAS

### 2.1 Operations side

**Facility / Property / Maintenance Manager**

Needs to know:

-   What is happening?
-   Why does it matter?
-   What should I do now?
-   Who owns the action?
-   Has anyone acknowledged it?
-   Did the intervention actually work?
-   Is the condition returning?

This persona may see detailed telemetry, asset state and operational
history.

### 2.2 Insurance risk side

**Loss-Control / Risk Engineer**

Needs to know:

-   Which recommendations remain open?
-   Which have reported completion only?
-   Which have independent verification?
-   What evidence supports verification?
-   Which previously verified improvements deteriorated?
-   Which sites need human follow-up?

### 2.3 Insurance decision side

**Underwriter**

Needs concise risk-quality evidence, not a sensor dashboard:

-   material open risks,
-   recommendation status,
-   verified improvements,
-   recurrence,
-   response behavior,
-   evidence confidence,
-   data sufficiency,
-   portfolio trend.

The underwriter remains responsible for underwriting decisions.

### 2.4 Portfolio side

**Broker / MGA / Corporate Risk Manager**

Needs:

-   multi-site recommendation status,
-   concentration of open risk,
-   aging actions,
-   verified improvements,
-   recurrence,
-   data coverage,
-   sites requiring intervention.

### 2.5 Trust side

**Security / Compliance / Model-Risk Reviewer**

Needs:

-   tenant isolation,
-   device identity,
-   evidence lineage,
-   deterministic state transitions,
-   AI governance,
-   consent history,
-   auditability,
-   retention controls,
-   explicit limitations.

# PART II --- NON-NEGOTIABLE DESIGN PRINCIPLES

## 3. SYSTEM PRINCIPLES

1.  **AI advises; deterministic services decide state; humans act;
    sensors verify.**
2.  **A human action report is evidence of action, not evidence of
    effectiveness.**
3.  **Missing, stale, unauthenticated or low-quality telemetry can never
    produce VERIFIED.**
4.  **Raw telemetry belongs to the operational side by default.**
5.  **Insurer access is evidence-oriented, consent-scoped, revocable and
    audited.**
6.  **No cloud-to-equipment control in v1.**
7.  **Risk logic uses canonical signals, never vendor or hardware model
    names.**
8.  **Every important state transition records policy/model version and
    evidence IDs.**
9.  **Synthetic/demo data is visibly labeled at API and UI level.**
10. **No failure silently becomes success.**
11. **AI output cannot create evidence, invent measurements, or directly
    mutate lifecycle state.**
12. **A previously verified improvement can deteriorate and reopen.**
13. **Every verification conclusion includes confidence/data-sufficiency
    information.**
14. **Modeled loss is scenario decision support, not proof of avoided
    loss.**
15. **The system must remain useful if Gemini is unavailable.**
16. **The simulator and physical hardware use the same ingestion and
    canonical contracts.**
17. **Build the simplest architecture that proves the loop; do not
    create microservices for presentation value.**
18. **Every external integration is behind an adapter/interface.**
19. **No personal/demographic variables are needed for risk detection or
    verification.**
20. **A carrier-facing score must never masquerade as an approved rating
    factor.**

# PART III --- CORE DOMAIN MODEL

## 4. THE PRIMARY AGGREGATE: RISK IMPROVEMENT CASE

A `RiskImprovementCase` is the durable business record.

``` ts
type RiskImprovementCase = {
  caseId: string;
  organizationId: string;
  facilityId: string;
  assetIds: string[];

  origin:
    | { type: "RECOMMENDATION"; recommendationId: string }
    | { type: "DETECTED_HAZARD"; detectionId: string }
    | { type: "MANUAL_RISK_REVIEW"; reviewId: string };

  hazardType: string;
  title: string;
  severity: "LOW" | "MODERATE" | "HIGH" | "CRITICAL";

  baselineSnapshotId?: string;
  activeRiskEventId?: string;

  assignedOwnerId?: string;
  targetDate?: string;

  state:
    | "OPEN"
    | "ACTION_REQUIRED"
    | "ACTION_REPORTED"
    | "VERIFYING"
    | "VERIFIED_IMPROVED"
    | "PARTIALLY_VERIFIED"
    | "NOT_IMPROVING"
    | "INCONCLUSIVE"
    | "CLOSED"
    | "REOPENED";

  latestVerificationId?: string;
  latestEvidencePackageId?: string;

  recurrenceCount: number;
  sharingState: "NOT_SHARED" | "SHAREABLE" | "SHARED" | "REVOKED";

  createdAt: string;
  updatedAt: string;
};
```

### 4.1 Why the case sits above an event

A single recommendation may produce:

-   multiple alerts,
-   multiple attempted interventions,
-   multiple verification windows,
-   a successful improvement,
-   later deterioration,
-   a reopened case.

The case preserves that full history.

## 5. RECOMMENDATION MODEL

Recommendations can originate from:

-   insurer/risk engineer,
-   facility risk team,
-   approved Symbiosis deterministic rule,
-   imported spreadsheet/API/file in a later version.

Minimum model:

``` ts
type RiskRecommendation = {
  recommendationId: string;
  organizationId: string;
  facilityId: string;
  assetIds: string[];
  source: "INSURER" | "FACILITY" | "SYMBIOSIS" | "IMPORTED";
  sourceReference?: string;
  hazardType: string;
  description: string;
  approvedActionIds: string[];
  targetDate?: string;
  verificationPolicyId?: string;
  status:
    | "OPEN"
    | "ACTION_REPORTED"
    | "VERIFYING"
    | "VERIFIED"
    | "PARTIAL"
    | "UNVERIFIED"
    | "CLOSED"
    | "REOPENED";
};
```

Do not equate `CLOSED` with `VERIFIED`. A customer may administratively
close something while evidence remains unverified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 6. RISK EVENT MODEL

A `RiskEvent` is a physical-risk episode within a case.

``` text
DETECTED
  → ALERTED
  → ACKNOWLEDGED
  → ACTION_REPORTED
  → VERIFYING
      ├─ VERIFIED
      ├─ PARTIALLY_VERIFIED
      ├─ NOT_IMPROVING
      └─ INCONCLUSIVE

No acknowledgement → ESCALATED
Readings normalize without reported action → SELF_RESOLVED
Authorized dismissal → DISMISSED_FALSE_ALARM
```

### 6.1 Event rules

-   Only deterministic lifecycle code changes state.
-   All transitions are idempotent.
-   Every transition writes an audit record.
-   User acknowledgement does not change physical-risk state.
-   `SELF_RESOLVED` provides no mitigation credit.
-   A new material recurrence after verification can create a new event
    and set the parent case to `REOPENED`.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

``` ts
type MitigationAction = {
```

## 8. VERIFICATION MODEL

``` ts
type VerificationAssessment = {
  verificationId: string;
  caseId: string;
  eventId: string;
  policyId: string;
  policyVersion: string;

  baselineWindow: TimeWindow;
  postActionWindow: TimeWindow;

  requiredCriteria: CriterionResult[];
  supportingCriteria: CriterionResult[];

  dataCompleteness: number;
  telemetryConfidence: number;
  deviceHealthStatus: string;
  authIntegrityStatus: string;

  result:
    | "VERIFIED"
    | "PARTIALLY_VERIFIED"
    | "NOT_IMPROVING"
    | "INCONCLUSIVE";

  confidence: number;
  evidenceIds: string[];
  evaluatedAt: string;
};
```

### 8.1 Verification rules

Verification is deterministic and policy-versioned.

A result can be `VERIFIED` only when:

-   all required criteria pass,
-   minimum observation count is met,
-   telemetry is fresh,
-   required devices are healthy,
-   device authentication/integrity requirements pass,
-   no disqualifying data-quality condition exists.

Insufficient evidence produces `INCONCLUSIVE`.

### 8.2 Persistence

A verification policy must define:

-   baseline window,
-   post-action window,
-   minimum observations,
-   required signals,
-   acceptable missingness,
-   sustained duration,
-   hysteresis,
-   recurrence watch window.

### 8.3 Reopen logic

After `VERIFIED_IMPROVED`, continued monitoring remains active.

If the same hazard materially returns during the configured recurrence
window:

``` text
VERIFIED_IMPROVED
  → recurrence detected
  → REOPENED
  → new RiskEvent
  → new action/verification cycle
```

This is a key product behavior.

``` ts
    | "relative_humidity"

    | "flow"
    | "outdoor_temperature";

  unit: string;

  observedAt: string;
  receivedAt: string;

  sourceType: "HARDWARE" | "SIMULATOR" | "BMS" | "OEM_API" | "WEATHER_API";

    stale: boolean;
    outOfRange: boolean;

```

Dedupe key:

``` text
device_id + signal + observed_at
```

# PART VI --- DETECTION AND VERIFICATION

## 15. HERO SCENARIO: COLD-STORAGE COOLING/ELECTRICAL RISK

The prototype simulates a degrading cooling/fan assembly.

### 15.1 Detection inputs

-   vibration RMS,
-   current draw,
-   zone temperature trend,
-   backup equipment state,
-   outdoor heat context from simulator/API,
-   device/data quality.

### 15.2 Initial deterministic rule

Example v1 rule:

``` text
vibration_z >= 2
AND current_deviation >= +8%
AND (
  outdoor_temperature >= 105°F
  OR zone_temperature_slope > 0
)
```

Severity/confidence depends on:

-   strength/persistence of signals,
-   number of corroborating signals,
-   telemetry confidence,
-   control availability.

A single abnormal signal creates at most a watch/moderate condition in
the demo.

### 15.3 Important hardware caveat

The SHT41 on a small bench may not produce a meaningful temperature
response over a short period. Therefore:

-   run the H4 bench experiment before locking temperature as required
    evidence;
-   if temperature response is weak, keep it as **supporting evidence**;
-   do not fake a temperature trend merely to satisfy the demo.

# PART VII --- AI ARCHITECTURE

## 18. GEMINI ROLE

Gemini may:

1.  explain the detected pathway in plain language,
2.  summarize trusted evidence,
3.  reorder/phrase actions from an approved action library,
4.  produce a concise underwriter/risk-engineer narrative,
5.  summarize portfolio patterns.

Gemini may not:

-   mark a case verified,
-   create measurements,
-   invent evidence,
-   close recommendations,
-   calculate a premium,
-   decide coverage,
-   autonomously control equipment,
-   write directly to lifecycle state.

### 18.1 Structured output

``` json
{
  "summary": "...",
  "risk_pathway": ["..."],
  "supporting_evidence_ids": ["EV-..."],
  "contradicting_evidence_ids": [],
  "missing_evidence": ["..."],
  "step_order": ["RA-..."],
  "confidence": 0.84
}
```

Reject and use deterministic fallback when:

-   invalid JSON/schema,
-   unknown evidence ID,
-   unknown action ID,
-   unsupported factual statement,
-   invalid confidence.

### 18.2 AI governance record

Log:

-   model name/version,
-   use case,
-   prompt-template version,
-   evidence IDs supplied,
-   validation outcome,
-   fallback status,
-   latency,
-   token/cost metadata,
-   timestamp.

The system remains operational without Gemini.

## 23A. RISK ENGINEER INTERVENTION / PRIORITIZATION

Symbiosis may produce a deterministic, explainable **intervention recommendation** to help insurer loss-control / risk-engineering teams prioritize where human attention is most useful.

This capability is **decision support, not automatic scheduling**. It does not dispatch a risk engineer, create an appointment, change underwriting state, or alter premium/coverage. A carrier or authorized human remains responsible for deciding whether, when and how a risk engineer engages.

### 23A.1 Intervention levels

``` text
REMOTE_MONITORING
REMOTE_REVIEW
RISK_ENGINEER_REVIEW
SITE_VISIT_RECOMMENDED
```

Human-readable UI labels:

- **Remote Monitoring** — continue observing; no immediate human review indicated by the configured policy.
- **Remote Review** — a loss-control/risk professional should review the case/evidence remotely.
- **Risk Engineer Review** — material conditions, uncertainty or recurrence warrant explicit risk-engineer assessment.
- **Site Visit Recommended** — available trusted evidence indicates that an on-site assessment should be considered.

### 23A.2 Inputs

The prioritization policy may use only trusted, explainable facts already available to the platform, including:

- case severity and hazard type,
- recommendation age / overdue status,
- verification result,
- repeated `NOT_IMPROVING`, `PARTIALLY_VERIFIED`, or `INCONCLUSIVE` outcomes,
- recurrence count and time since recurrence,
- material deterioration after a prior verified improvement,
- response / acknowledgement behavior,
- telemetry completeness and confidence,
- device/authentication integrity,
- corroborating physical signals,
- external contextual signals such as weather when available through an approved adapter,
- number and concentration of open material risks at the site,
- inability to establish sufficient remote evidence.

It must not use personal/demographic attributes or an LLM-generated assertion as a deciding input.

### 23A.3 Deterministic policy

The intervention level is produced by a **versioned deterministic policy**, not by Gemini.

Illustrative policy behavior:

``` text
Low/moderate condition + trusted monitoring + improving/stable state
  → REMOTE_MONITORING

Evidence needs professional interpretation but remote evidence is sufficient
  → REMOTE_REVIEW

Repeated recurrence / material unresolved recommendation /
repeated NOT_IMPROVING or consequential uncertainty
  → RISK_ENGINEER_REVIEW

Critical/material unresolved risk + insufficient remote verification /
repeated recurrence / required physical inspection condition
  → SITE_VISIT_RECOMMENDED
```

Exact thresholds and combinations belong in versioned configuration, for example:

``` text
config/intervention-policy/risk-engineer-prioritization.v1.json
```

They must not be hard-coded in UI components.

### 23A.4 Output contract

``` ts
type RiskEngineerInterventionRecommendation = {
  interventionId: string;
  organizationId: string;
  facilityId: string;
  caseId?: string;

  level:
    | "REMOTE_MONITORING"
    | "REMOTE_REVIEW"
    | "RISK_ENGINEER_REVIEW"
    | "SITE_VISIT_RECOMMENDED";

  policyId: string;
  policyVersion: string;
  reasonCodes: string[];
  supportingEvidenceIds: string[];
  dataSufficiency: number;
  generatedAt: string;
  status: "ACTIVE" | "ACKNOWLEDGED" | "RESOLVED" | "SUPERSEDED";
};
```

Every recommendation must expose its reason codes and supporting evidence. Missing or low-quality evidence must not be silently converted into confidence; where configured, insufficient remote evidence may itself justify escalation for human review.

### 23A.5 AI boundary

Gemini may translate the deterministic recommendation into plain language, for example:

> "Site visit recommended because the critical cooling risk has recurred twice after a previously verified improvement and the latest verification window lacks sufficient trusted current data."

Gemini may **not** choose or change the intervention level.

### 23A.6 Lifecycle behavior

Intervention prioritization is recalculated on material events such as:

``` text
risk.detected
verification.completed
recommendation.overdue
recurrence.detected
telemetry.quality_changed
device.health_changed
case.reopened
```

A new calculation may downgrade or escalate the recommendation according to the current policy. Historical recommendations remain auditable.

### 23A.7 Portfolio use

The Risk Evidence Workspace may use this capability to answer:

- Which sites can remain under remote monitoring?
- Which cases need remote professional review?
- Which sites should a risk engineer review first?
- Which cases have insufficient evidence for remote verification?
- Which recurring or unresolved risks warrant consideration of an on-site visit?

This is intended to help loss-control teams focus limited human attention on cases where it is most useful while preserving human authority over actual engagement and scheduling.

    as simulator data;

3.  a human can take a physical action;
